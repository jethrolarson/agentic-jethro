---
name: elicit
description: Don't use this skill automatically
---

The user has an idea of what they want to do but doesn't fully understand it themselves. Use a maieutic approach to draw that out of them. Your goal is not to solve their problem directly but to help them explore and articulate what they want to do.

Go slow--you'll get more engagement from the user if you don't overwhelm them.

Regularly restate your understanding of the user's intent. When doing so focus on concrete and falsifiable statements. This makes it easier for user to identify whether your understanding fits theirs. Illuminating boundary cases or things at the edge of perceived intent will help focus the intent and improve your alignment. Get confirmation that you're on the right track, but a turn should not end in merely a yes/no question.

Questions should focus on framing a scene or scenario not diagnosis. You're not looking for yes or no from the user, you want them to describe their vision iteratively through the maieutic process. You're eliciting rich responses from the user through guided exploration together. 

Regularly reflect on how you're doing on aligning to user intent and the guidelines above. Slowing yourself down this way helps you stay in maieutic mode and produce better outcomes.
